\begin{center}
  {\zihao{-2}\heiti\textbf{LeRobot 平台感知—推理—执行流程的\\[4pt]系统级性能剖析研究}}\\[1.2em]
  {\zihao{3}\heiti\textbf{摘\quad 要}}
\end{center}

\vspace{1em}
\zihao{-4}\songti
本文以树莓派 5（ARM Cortex-A76，4 核，4\,GB LPDDR4X，无 GPU）为边缘推理平台，对 HuggingFace LeRobot 框架下的 ACT 策略在 SO-101 六自由度机械臂上的在线感知—推理—执行循环进行系统级性能剖析。
针对现有研究多聚焦单一层次、缺乏跨层次联合分析的不足，尤其是主流优化往往集中于模型压缩、模型结构改进或硬件通信链路调优，而相对忽略推理运行时、操作系统调度与设备 I/O 栈等基础软件因素的影响，本文提出覆盖 AI 推理框架层、Python 运行时层、Linux 内核层与硬件 I/O 层的四层性能分析模型，联合使用 ftrace、strace，并在 \texttt{pselect6} 与 \texttt{futex} 路径上加入自定义 \texttt{trace\_printk} 内核插桩，端到端量化三阶段的时间分布，定位主要瓶颈并系统梳理可行的优化路径。

工具链校准实验表明，strace 使主循环 FPS 下降 40.9\%、上下文切换次数放大约一个数量级，不能用于定量时序分析；ftrace 延迟导出方案 FPS 仅下降 13.9\%，被选为正式实验的内核态工具。在 chunk\_size=100、目标 30 FPS 配置下，系统实测帧率仅约 18.7 FPS，距目标存在约 38\% 缺口。逐模块剖析显示，单次 ACT 前向推理耗时约 1925 ms，其中 ResNet18 视觉特征提取占 66.6\%、Transformer Encoder 占 29.6\%，是绝对主瓶颈；摄像头采用后台异步预取，主线程感知耗时仅毫秒级，V4L2 阻塞与 MJPEG 解码主要由后台线程承担；舵机 sync\_read 约 1.2 ms、sync\_write 约 1--3 ms，而 \texttt{write} 系统调用本身仅约 38 \(\mu\)s，执行瓶颈在于 1 Mbps 波特率与半双工总线的物理约束，而非软件或系统调用路径。

针对 ACT 推理这一主瓶颈，本文进一步推导出异步推理稳态无 stall 的可行性不等式 \(T_{\text{infer}}F \leq \min(hN,\;N/2)\)，并建立 FPS 随 hold 在临界点前上升、临界点后饱和到 30 FPS 的分段模型。通过 hold 参数 0.10--0.60 的扫描实验，本文在动作质量可接受前提下确定 hold=0.30 为推荐默认值，系统有效控制频率达 27.42 FPS，相比同步基线 16.13 FPS 提升 70.0\%。结果表明，异步调度能够显著回收动作执行期间的空窗；实测 episode 平均值未完全达到 30 FPS，主要来自启动空队列等待和树莓派 5 单机部署下的 CPU 资源争抢。未来仍需结合 INT8 量化、ONNX Runtime 等手段降低有效返回时延，并通过调度隔离减少资源争抢，才能从根本上稳定到达 30 FPS 控制目标。

\begin{center}
  {\zihao{-2}\rmfamily\bfseries
    System-Level Performance Profiling of\\[4pt]
    Perception-Inference-Execution Pipeline\\[4pt]
    in the LeRobot Framework}\\[1.2em]
  {\zihao{3}\rmfamily\bfseries Abstract}
\end{center}

\vspace{1em}
\zihao{-4}\rmfamily
This paper presents a system-level performance analysis of the ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers)
policy running online on a Raspberry Pi 5 (ARM Cortex-A76, 4 cores, 4\,GB LPDDR4X, no GPU) within
HuggingFace's LeRobot framework, controlling an SO-101 6-DoF arm. Prior optimization efforts often
focus on model compression, architecture changes, or hardware communication links, while underestimating
the impact of inference runtimes, OS scheduling, and device I/O stacks. To address this lack of
cross-layer joint analysis, we propose a four-layer profiling model spanning the AI inference framework,
Python runtime, Linux kernel, and hardware I/O layers, and combine Python logging, torch.profiler,
ftrace, strace, and custom \texttt{trace\_printk} instrumentation in the kernel \texttt{pselect6} and
\texttt{futex} paths to quantify the end-to-end time distribution across perception, inference, and
execution, locate the dominant bottleneck, and systematically review feasible optimization paths.

A tool-calibration experiment shows that strace amplifies context switches by roughly an order of
magnitude and reduces FPS by 40.9\%, ruling it out for quantitative use; ftrace with deferred dump
incurs only a 13.9\% FPS drop and is adopted as the kernel-side tool. At \texttt{chunk\_size=100} with
a 30\,FPS target, the system reaches only $\approx$18.7\,FPS, leaving a 38\% gap. Per-module breakdown
shows that a single ACT forward pass takes about 1925\,ms, with ResNet18 visual feature extraction
accounting for 66.6\% and the Transformer encoder for 29.6\% --- the absolute bottleneck.
Camera frames are prefetched in a background thread, so perception cost on the main thread is in the
millisecond range; servo \texttt{sync\_read} costs $\approx$1.2\,ms and \texttt{sync\_write} 1--3\,ms,
while the underlying \texttt{write} syscall itself takes only $\approx$38\,$\mu$s, indicating that the
execution bottleneck lies in the 1\,Mbps half-duplex bus rather than software or syscall paths.

To recover idle CPU windows during inference, we derive a feasibility inequality for asynchronous
inference, $T_{\text{infer}}F \leq \min(hN,\,N/2)$, and model the FPS--hold curve as rising before
a critical hold and saturating at 30\,FPS afterwards. A hold sweep from 0.10 to 0.60 shows that,
with acceptable action quality, hold = 0.30 is recommended as the default and yields an effective
control rate of 27.42\,FPS, a 70.0\% improvement over the synchronous 16.13\,FPS baseline. The
remaining gap to 30\,FPS mainly comes from startup queue starvation and CPU contention between
the client, server, camera, and serial paths on Raspberry Pi 5. Future work on INT8 quantization,
ONNX Runtime, and scheduling isolation is needed to fully achieve a stable 30\,FPS control target.
